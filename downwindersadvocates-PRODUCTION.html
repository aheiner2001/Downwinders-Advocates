Before anything else

Let's get the uncomfortable part out of the way.

You are probably wondering if this is a scam. You should be. After the program expanded, a lot of outfits started cold-calling families and knocking on doors on tribal land. The New Mexico Attorney General and the Tularosa Basin Downwinders Consortium both put out warnings about it. If someone has already called you about this out of nowhere, you were right to be suspicious.

You are probably wondering if we are the government. We are not. We are a private company. We are not the Department of Justice and we are not a law firm.

And you are probably wondering why anyone should pay for something you heard is free. Here is the honest answer. You can file this claim yourself, for free. RESEP clinics will help you for free. Plenty of people do it alone and do it fine. We are a paid convenience for families who would rather not gather forty years of records themselves, and we do not get paid unless the claim gets paid.

If any of that means we are not for you, that is a completely reasonable place to land.

The 19 cancers covered for downwinders

Leukemia other than chronic lymphocytic leukemia, multiple myeloma, lymphomas other than Hodgkin's disease, and primary cancer of the thyroid, male or female breast, esophagus, stomach, pharynx, small intestine, pancreas, bile ducts, gall bladder, salivary gland, urinary bladder, brain, colon, ovary, liver (except where cirrhosis or hepatitis B is indicated), or lung.

Uranium miners, millers and ore transporters have a different list of seven: lung cancer, pulmonary fibrosis, cor pulmonale related to fibrosis of the lung, silicosis, pneumoconiosis, renal cancer, and other chronic renal disease including nephritis and kidney tubal tissue injury. Four of those seven are not cancer.

Manhattan Project waste claims use a third list of 21, which adds bone cancer and renal cancer, and the illness must have begun at least two years after exposure.

Questions

The things people actually ask

Is this free? Why would I pay you?

You can file it yourself at no cost, and RESEP clinics will help you for free. We are for families who would rather not spend three to six months chasing residency and employment records from the sixties and seventies. If you have the time and patience for that, do it yourself and keep the money.

How much is the compensation?

Up to $100,000, depending on the category. Manhattan Project waste claimants receive $50,000 or $25,000. It is one-time and it is not taxed. No one can tell you in advance what you will receive, or whether you will receive anything.

Which years does someone have to have lived there?

For downwinders, at least one year of physical presence between January 21, 1951 and November 6, 1962, or the entire period of June 30 to July 31, 1962. New Mexico reaches back further, to September 24, 1944, because of the Trinity test. Someone who moved to a covered county after November 1962 does not qualify as a downwinder. Uranium workers are a separate category and these dates do not apply to them.

What is the deadline?

Claims must be filed with the Department of Justice on or before December 31, 2027. That is the filing deadline, not the starting deadline, and the gathering is the part that takes months.

My father already passed. Is it too late?

No. Surviving spouses, children, parents, and in some cases grandchildren can file. This is the single most common thing families get wrong, and it costs them the whole claim.

Which illnesses are covered?

There are three separate lists. Downwinders have 19 covered cancers, uranium workers have 7 conditions of which 4 are not cancer, and Manhattan Project waste claims have 21. Kidney and bladder are not the same category. Melanoma never qualifies. Uranium workers are also covered for nonmalignant lung conditions like silicosis and pulmonary fibrosis. If anyone told you "any cancer counts," they were wrong or they were selling.

How long does it take?

Federal processing generally runs six to twelve months from a complete filing. There is an expedited path for documented terminal diagnoses.

I was already denied.

A denial is not always the end. Sometimes it is a documentation gap rather than an eligibility problem. We will look at what came back at no cost and tell you honestly whether there is anything there.

Are you the government?

No. We are a private company. Not the DOJ, not any federal agency, and not a law firm. Nobody from the government is going to call you about this program. If someone says they are, hang up.
